Al-Saad, 100 MW: six questions a bidder should be able to answer before 8 November

Seif Hemdan · 4 November 2026

Bids for HUMAIN's 100 MW AI data centre at Al-Saad, Riyadh, are due on 8 November 2026. I have not seen the RFP; it is not public. What follows rests on the trade reporting of it, with every number checked against a dated source on 4 November 2026.

Nothing in the reported design is wrong for the hardware shipping today. The question is what the first hall opens into. It is due for service in October 2028; NVIDIA's Kyber rack, last put at about 600 kW, is now expected the same year; the tender reportedly specifies 240–250 kW per rack.

The reported air-cooled chillers, meanwhile, will carry real load for five to seven months of the Riyadh year. How much depends on one number the reporting does not give: the facility water temperature. How much the hall can take later depends on another: whether floor space and busway are reserved for 800 V DC power sidecars.

A bidder who can answer the six questions at the end has priced both. One who cannot is carrying both as open risk.

MEED gives the transformers, the 15 UPS units and the mechanical UPS "for each 25MW block": 15 × 2,250 kW is 33.75 MW of static UPS against 25 MW of IT load, consistent with redundant sizing. That ratio's resemblance to the 1.35 PUE target is coincidence; the two figures are unrelated.

The campus this may be the first building of

MEED's project database lists a separate HUMAIN scheme in the same area: a 6 GW AI data-centre campus on a 24 km² site in the Al-Saad area, about 130 km east of Riyadh, in two phases across six 1 GW plots. The plan was announced on 3 December 2025; Al Yamama was appointed main contractor for the infrastructure works on 25 September 2026, with Egis, Hatch, JLL and Turner & Townsend as consultants and PMO (MEED Projects profile, last updated September 2026, opened 4 November 2026).

The reporting on the 100 MW tender does not tie it to that campus, and I have not seen a document that does. But the area is the same, the infrastructure award came eight days after the 100 MW tender was issued, and a 100 MW hall is the natural first building on a 6 GW site. If that is what it is, the answers to the six questions below are not a one-off. They are the template for what follows, and every rack-density, loop-temperature and 800 V DC decision made in this bid gets copied.

The hardware gap lands at opening, not years later

A hall built for 250 kW positions opens in the year 600 kW-class racks are expected to ship.

NVIDIA showed its Kyber rack at GTC in March 2025 as Rubin Ultra NVL576, a name it has since changed to Kyber NVL144 under its package count. Jensen Huang put it at up to about 600 kW per rack in a GTC question-and-answer session; that is not a published specification (Tom's Hardware, 19 Mar 2025). SemiAnalysis still works its example at 600 kW and puts Kyber Ultra near 660 kW (26 May 2026). NVIDIA's own developer blog describes the 800 V sidecar shown at GTC 2025 to power 576 Rubin Ultra GPUs in one Kyber rack (20 May 2025).

Two caveats. The Kyber shown at GTC 2026 had half the compute sleds, and NVIDIA has not restated the rack's power rating (Glenn Lockwood, Apr 2026). And the rack has slipped: planned for the second half of 2027, it moved more than 12 months to 2028 over a hard-to-manufacture midplane, and the NVL72x2 stopgap was dropped (Tom's Hardware, 6 Jul 2026, reporting SemiAnalysis). NVIDIA's reply, on 15 July 2026, was that its roadmap is intact.

On either schedule, Kyber-class racks are expected to be shipping by, or within months of, phase 1's opening in October 2028 and phase 2's in January 2029. A hall built for 250 kW positions should expect to be asked for 600 kW-class racks from its first year.

What that means physically: at 250 kW, a 25 MW hall holds about 100 rack positions; at 600 kW, the same 25 MW feeds about 42. The building's total power does not change; each position must carry about 2.4 times more power and coolant. For water with a 10 K temperature rise, that is roughly 6 litres a second per rack today and 14 at 600 kW.

Pipework, coolant distribution units and busway sized for 250 kW per position are not simply extended to reach 600 kW; they are re-engineered or replaced. Kyber's own sidecar racks also take floor positions. NVIDIA has not published the rack's weight, so floor loading is an open question.

The 800 V DC upgrade has an estimated price floor

Making the halls ready for 800 V DC power racks is a 4–5% question on this contract, if the space for them exists.

SemiAnalysis estimates an 800 V DC power rack at $400–500k per unit, about ten times the roughly $40k of a standard AC power rack, or near $500k per deployed MW, and puts the retrofit delta at roughly $400–500k per MW, mostly the power rack (26 May 2026). Applied to Al-Saad:

Scope Power-rack estimate Share of the \~$1bn EPC estimate
100 MW (phases 1 and 2) about $40–50m about 4–5%
200 MW (with phase 3) about $80–100m not comparable; the value of phase 3 has not been reported

This is a floor: power racks only, not busway, switchgear, space or downtime. NVIDIA says 800 V DC supports incremental adoption, with rack-level power racks for hybrid integration in existing facilities (NVIDIA, 800 VDC architecture, undated). Fiisual's reading of NVIDIA's white paper has it deployed at rack or row level while AC and DC distribution coexist during the transition (17 Aug 2026).

The cost of that path turns almost entirely on whether floor space and busway routes for sidecars are reserved now, in the bid. The public coverage does not say.

The reported chillers carry real load for five to seven months, and the water temperature decides how much

The chillers are already in the design; the unreported facility water temperature decides whether they run on summer afternoons or on most days of the year.

MEED reports direct-to-chip loops paired with air-cooled chillers in dual high- and low-temperature loops, alongside fan-wall units (16 Oct 2026). So the question is not whether chillers exist but how much of the heat they carry on a Riyadh afternoon.

NVIDIA's Vera Rubin generation is designed for 45 °C inlet water. "The water that goes into it is the same temperature, 45 degrees C," Jensen Huang said at CES 2026, adding that at 45 °C "no water chillers are necessary" (Fierce Network, 7 Jan 2026). Dell'Oro's Alex Cordovil replied that nobody wants a data centre to go down when a heat wave strikes. In Riyadh the heat wave is most of the summer.

Working back with GR1D's design reasoning (15 Oct 2026): 45 °C at the chip, minus about 4 K across the coolant distribution unit, gives about 41 °C facility water. A dry cooler needs a further 5–8 K, so outside air must be at or below about 33–36 °C for dry cooling to carry the load alone. GR1D also notes that Vera Rubin NVL72 racks are specified up to 250 kW on 45 °C coolant, the density the tender reports.

If the hardware needs 30 °C water instead, which Schneider Electric's Andrew Bradner says chips above 500 W require, falling to about 27 °C at 700 W (DCD, "Hot water, cold water", undated online), the dry-cooling ceiling drops to about 18–21 °C outside.

From May to September, with April and October at the edge, Riyadh's average daily high sits above the ceiling for 45 °C water: chillers carry part of the heat on most of those afternoons. July's mean night-time low is 27.3 °C (Climates to Travel, 1991–2010), so dry coolers can carry the load for part of the night. Against the ceiling for 30 °C water, only January (20.7 °C) comes close, with December at 22.3 °C; chillers would run for part of most days of the year.

The tender reportedly asks for an annualised PUE below 1.35 (MEED, 16 Oct 2026). One vendor guide puts warm-water liquid cooling at 1.20–1.35 in climates that need supplemental chilling (Rax, 11 Sep 2026), so the annual figure is plausible. But it is an average: the cool months mask summer afternoons when the chillers add a large load.

Those hours coincide with Saudi Arabia's summer grid peak, a record 77.08 GW in 2025 (MEES, 1 May 2026). Peak-hour power sizes the grid connection and the backup; the annual PUE does not show it.

Cautions

  • The 600 kW figure is "up to", given in a question-and-answer session, and NVIDIA did not restate it at GTC 2026. A second slip would push Kyber past phase 2 and make the 250 kW hall the right design for longer. I found no newer public statement of the rack's rating or date as of 4 November 2026.
  • The 33–36 °C and 18–21 °C ceilings come from one vendor's design reasoning. A larger dry cooler buys a smaller approach; a fouled one loses it. Each ceiling is a band that moves a few degrees with equipment selection and maintenance, not a fixed line.
  • 45 °C is the chip-inlet maximum NVIDIA states, not a design point. Designing at the maximum leaves no margin for a heat wave, fouling or a degraded pump; that is Cordovil's point, and it is mine too.
  • Monthly normals understate afternoons. The design afternoon is 44.2 °C against a July average high of 43.8 °C, and the extreme annual maximum is 45.8 °C. A weather-file simulation, not a monthly chart, is what sizes the plant.
  • The 70–80% liquid share is a reference-design figure; a Rubin-class rack may push more of its heat to liquid. Whatever the share, if the low-temperature loop runs at a conventional chilled-water temperature, the fan-wall heat is on chillers for almost every daytime hour of the Riyadh year. The reported loop temperatures are not public.
  • The chiller draw is a bounding illustration from one chiller's nominal rating, not a plant design. The warm loop sees a much smaller lift, and there the chillers only trim.
  • The \~$1bn is a press estimate that MEED's own text does not state, and the $200m first-phase award is a different scope on a different site. The per-MW figures show the order of magnitude, nothing finer.

  • The 800 V DC delta is SemiAnalysis's estimate for new power racks. A retrofit also pays for busway, switchgear changes, floor space and downtime, and NVIDIA's 2027 production plan was tied to Kyber, which has moved to 2028.

  • The 6 GW campus is a separate MEED Projects listing. The link between it and the 100 MW tender is my inference from the shared area and the dates, not a reported fact, and the profile's own value fields are blank or inconsistent, so I take only its location, structure, names and dates from it.

Six questions a bidder should be able to answer

Some of these the RFP sets and the bidder reads; the rest the bidder designs and prices. Each turns a risk into a number before 8 November.

  1. What facility water supply temperature does the specification require, and was it set for 45 °C or 30 °C class chips?
  2. At what outdoor design temperature is the chiller plant sized, and what share of peak heat does it carry at that temperature?
  3. Is floor space and a busway route reserved for 800 V DC sidecars in each hall, and at what rack positions?
  4. Are row-level pipework and coolant distribution units sized for 250 kW, or for an upgrade to 600 kW per position?
  5. Is the PUE below 1.35 guaranteed on a named Riyadh weather file, and is there a separate summer-peak power guarantee?
  6. What floor loading does the structure allow per rack position, and does it cover heavier next-generation racks?

The first and third decide most of what the other four cost.

What I have not seen

  • The RFP itself. It is not public; every specification above is as reported.
  • The facility water supply temperature, and the split of heat between dry coolers and chillers. Neither is reported.
  • A current ASHRAE design table for Riyadh. The 2005 edition's table was the latest I could open; the 2021 and 2025 editions exist and I could not open them. I have not run a weather-file simulation of hourly chiller load or summer-peak PUE.
  • The full cost of an 800 V DC retrofit beyond the power-rack floor.
  • Kyber's rack weight and coolant specifications. NVIDIA has not published them.

  • Any extension to the 8 November bid date. None had been published as of 4 November 2026.

  • A document that ties the 100 MW tender to the 6 GW Al-Saad campus.
